## 3. Current pipeline behavior

1. **Parse and normalize** timestamped screenplay events and canonicalize series aliases.
2. **Generate windows** from real dialogue/action boundaries.
3. **Reject invalid windows** using deterministic timing, dialogue-integrity, fragment, overlap, and continuation rules.
4. **Shortlist per episode** (normally 4–5 candidates in series mode).
5. **Score narrative quality** using Gemini, validated no-API JSON, or the explicitly lower-quality rule engine.
6. **Rank globally** using the displayed ranking adjustment, a quality threshold, variety constraints, and a maximum of two selected promos per episode.
7. **Inspect and export** the ranked list; human approval is optional until an editor decides to produce a video.

## 5. Scoring and ranking shown in the UI

### Rule score

For offline rule scoring, `scoreWindow` in `screenplayEngine.ts` calculates:

- Hook score
- Cliffhanger score
- Standalone clarity
- Duration fit, with the best fit at 40 seconds
- Raw decision blend: 35% hook + 35% cliffhanger + 20% standalone + 10% duration fit

These heuristic values are rough editorial ratings, not calibrated probabilities.

### Series ranking adjustment

`rankAllEpisodesGlobally` applies the exact adjustment displayed on every promo card:

| Episode range | Adjustment |
|---|---:|
| EP_001–EP_015 | +5 |
| EP_016–EP_030 | +2 |
| EP_031–EP_039 | 0 |
| EP_040 onward | -6 plus `late-series spoiler risk` |

The **Ranking Score** equals the raw decision score plus this adjustment, clamped to 0–100. The frontend shows all three fields—Ranking Score, Raw decision blend, and Series adjustment—so displayed ordering is explainable.

Series mode defaults to:

- Quality threshold: 70
- Top limit: 25
- Maximum per episode: 2
- At least ten candidates retained when the scored pool has ten or more, even if the threshold would leave fewer
- No conflicting overlapping or immediate question/answer continuation pair in the final list
- No more than two candidates of one moment type in the first five positions

Episode mode returns at most the top three candidates from that episode.

## 6. How the product marks a potentially bad final pick

The promo card's **Final check** is a deterministic presentation rule in `View3RankedPromos.tsx`:

- **Advance** — Ranking Score is at least 80 and none of the major automated risks are present.
- **Review risk** — Ranking Score is below 80 or exactly one major automated risk is present.
- **Hold — weak/risky pick** — Ranking Score is below 70 or two or more major automated risks are present.

Major automated risks are:

- `soft hook`
- `possible fake cliffhanger`
- `needs context`
- `late-series spoiler risk`

This label is decision support, not a new model score. Hard violations are rejected before ranking; the final label helps a reviewer understand why a surviving candidate may still be unsuitable for production.

## 7. Evidence and reasoning

Each ranked promo contains source-grounded:

- Hook line, speaker, and timestamp
- Cliffhanger line, speaker, and timestamp
- Transcript events inside the cut
- An editorial explanation covering the opening, a middle escalation beat, the unresolved ending, and context risk
- H1–H9 pass labels
- Quality-risk flags

Model instructions require 2–4 evidence-grounded sentences and forbid invented dialogue, timestamps, candidates, or confidence values (`backend/app/api/demo.py`). Rule-only reasons cite the exact opening and an escalation event selected from the cut (`screenplayEngine.ts`).

The character widgets use only canonical speaker names and dialogue counts. They generate initials and styling locally; they do not invent faces, biographies, or relationships.

## 8. Model use, security, and swapping

The public frontend exposes these scoring options (`src/config.ts`):

1. **Google Gemini (server-managed key)** — default model name `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`.
2. **Rule-based scoring (lower quality, offline)**.
3. **No-API prompt + JSON paste** — pauses the run, validates the pasted schema and candidate IDs, then resumes.

There is no browser API-key field. The frontend does not send an `api_key` property. Gemini credentials must be stored in `backend/.env` locally or encrypted deployment environment variables such as Vercel's `GEMINI_API_KEY`.

## 9. Upload, privacy, and persistence

- Accepts `.txt`, folders, and `.zip` series.
- Per-screenplay limit shown in the UI: 5 MB.
- Batch limit shown in the UI: 100 episodes / 50 MB unpacked.
- ZIP entries are read in browser memory; no filesystem extraction is required.
- Invalid UTF-8, unsupported files, unsafe ZIP content, duplicate IDs, and partial parse failures are surfaced explicitly.
- Uploaded scripts and session library entries remain in browser session storage for the demo.
- The repository/public demo contains invented sample content, not the company screenplay pack (`CONTENT_NOTICE.md`).
- A shared multi-user deployment would require authenticated durable storage; the current frontend does not claim that a database exists.

### Why Stage 3 provides several files

The export stage serves three different consumers: the campaign/editorial reviewer, the video editor, and the technical auditor. The files are not duplicates:

| File or button | Intended user | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| **Full Run ZIP** | Submission reviewer / archive owner | Packages the complete, reproducible run in one download, including tables, timelines, run metadata, and raw model evidence. |
| `promos_master.csv` / **Master CSV** | Producer, campaign manager, spreadsheet reviewer | One row per promo segment with ranks, exact timings, scores, adjustments, evidence lines, reasoning, flags, and review status. Best for sorting and comparing the whole campaign. |
| `cut_list.csv` / **Cut List CSV** | Assistant editor | A minimal conform-oriented list of promo ID, episode, in/out time, and duration. It intentionally omits narrative detail so it can be handed directly to an editing workflow. |
| `promos.json` / **JSON Cut Sheet** | Developer, automation, downstream service | The lossless machine-readable result: run metadata, full promo objects, score components, evidence, transcript lines, flags, and segments. CSV cannot preserve this nested structure. |
| `report.md` / **Report MD** | Human reviewer | A readable explanation of ranking order and each promo's evidence, including raw score, series adjustment, final ranking score, hook, cliffhanger, reasoning, and risks. |
| `promos.edl` / **EDL Timeline** | Premiere, Resolve, Avid-style editorial workflows | CMX-style timeline events for rapid conform. It is a screenplay-derived plan and still requires footage validation. |
| `promos.otio` / **OTIO Timeline** | Modern NLE and pipeline interchange | OpenTimelineIO preserves structured clip/range metadata more reliably than a flat EDL for compatible tools. |
| `run_log.json` | Evaluator / engineer | Records the exact run configuration, provider/model identity, usage when available, check summary, timings when measured, and errors. This makes claims traceable. |
| `TIMELINE_NOTES.txt` | Video editor | Prevents misuse by stating that timeline files were generated from screenplay timestamps and were not conformed against final footage. |
| `raw/model_response_*.json` | Auditor / model evaluator | Preserves the exact model output unedited, allowing score and reasoning claims to be checked against what the provider actually returned. Rule-only runs include `raw/README.txt` instead. |
| **Single-promo JSON** | Editor working on one cut | Sends one selected promo and its full evidence without requiring the recipient to parse the complete run. |
| **Selected-promos JSON** | Campaign producer | Exports only human-approved selections for the next production stage while preserving their evidence and cut specifications. |

For a simple submission, the reviewer normally opens `report.md` and `promos_master.csv`; the editor uses `cut_list.csv`, EDL, or OTIO; and the ZIP/JSON/raw files provide reproducibility and audit evidence.

## 11. Human role

Human review is optional during discovery. The model/rule pipeline can rank candidates, provide evidence, and recommend **Advance**, **Review risk**, or **Hold** without requiring two blinded editors.

A human becomes necessary before production when deciding campaign fit, checking actor delivery/tone against footage, adjusting line boundaries, and approving the final video. Accept/reject actions remain available but do not block discovery or export.

## 12. Known limits

1. Screenplay-only analysis cannot assess actor delivery, facial expression, music, sound design, or edited visual pacing.
2. Script timestamps may not match final footage; EDL/OTIO files require conform validation.
3. Rule scores are heuristic, and model scores are ordinal judgments rather than measurements.
4. Irony, sarcasm, and relationship context can remain ambiguous without audio/video.
5. Gold-set hit rate, precision, recall, and reviewer agreement remain **Not measured**.
6. The session library is not durable multi-user storage.
7. The current audit scores the rule-shortlisted candidates rather than every raw window with Gemini.
