1. Problem Choice & Operational Framing

I chose Problem 1 (Find the Promos) because discovering high-converting promotional cut points is the single most urgent operational bottleneck in vertical drama streaming. While other challenges drift toward speculative end-to-end video synthesis, promo selection is an immediate, business-critical editorial workflow with clear evaluation criteria:

Why Problem 1? (Strategic & Engineering Rationale)

  • Immediate Operational Bottleneck: Editing teams manually comb through hours of episodic drama to find 30–60s acquisition hooks for TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts under weekly release deadlines.
  • Directly Measurable Business Impact: Top-of-funnel promo retention and click-through directly drive initial coin unlocks and subscriber acquisition curves.
  • Realistic Series-Scale Verifiability: The 50 supplied episodes (~3,727 events) permit rigorous full-series stress testing against real-world production scale rather than isolated toy demos.
  • Clean Division of Labor: High-precision deterministic code guarantees timing and boundary physics, while AI evaluates narrative tension, dramatic pacing, and curiosity gaps.
  • Scoped for Editorial Utility: Focuses on solving candidate discovery and NLE cut-sheet generation rather than ungrounded “virality” hype or synthetic video hallucinations.

Hard Editorial Invariants & Operating Boundaries

  • Duration Bounds: Strictly bounded to 20.0s – 60.0s total runtime.
  • Dialogue Boundary Validation: Zero cuts inside spoken lines (<0.1s tolerance against script event clocks; text-only boundary check).
  • Curiosity Hook & Cliffhanger: Opening hook in first 3.0s; ends on maximum unresolved curiosity/tension without answering its own premise.
  • Confidentiality Guarantee: Zero proprietary screenplay dialogue committed to public git or leaked to public models.
  • Human-in-the-Loop Workflow: AI proposes, ranks, and annotates; editors retain final review, boundary trimming, and NLE export control.

2. System Architecture: Five-Layer Runtime & Division of Labor

Design principle: deterministic code owns correctness, AI evaluates narrative meaning, and editors retain creative control.
Browser / TypeScript: Owns integer-ms timing, boundary validation (<0.1s), candidate generation, shortlist filtering, and NLE export encoding.
FastAPI Backend: Owns /api/* routing, Pydantic schema validation, secure credential isolation (zero browser keys), and error handling.
Google Gemini (Flash Lite): Owns semantic narrative scoring (hook potency, cliffhanger urgency, standalone clarity) — zero arithmetic duty.
Human Editor: Owns final creative oversight, AI disagreement audits, line-boundary trim nudging, and production publish approvals.

Failure Mode Retrospective & Applied Fixes

  • 1. Standalone Disagreement Bug: 17 of 25 promos had high AI standalone scores (≥80) but were flagged with heuristic "needs context" because regexes flagged missing pronouns. Fix: Reconciled in UI by surfacing Context Review Needed (AI Disagreement) with an explicit explanatory tooltip instead of corrupting the score.
  • 2. EP_027 Duplicate 5.4s Shared Beat: Two cuts in EP_027 shared 5.4s of a confrontation beat because relative overlap was 14.7% ≤ 20%. Fix: Enforced an absolute ≥4.0s overlap threshold alongside relative >15%, eliminating duplicate beat reuse across all episodes.
  • 3. Quiet Romance Episode Infiltration: Low-stakes domestic conversations (EP_006, EP_014, EP_017) snuck into rule ranks. Fix: Implemented soft dialogue damping for clips with romance markers and zero escalation, eliminating them from the top 25 global promos.
  • 4. Editorial Counter Overwrite: Approving a single promo in the UI overwrote engine candidate counts. Fix: Separated engine inventory (candidates_accepted) from human curation counts (user_approved, user_rejected, user_pending).

5. Tools Used & Where AI Got It Wrong

Tooling Stack

TypeScript 5.7, React 19, Tailwind CSS v4, Vite 8, Python 3.12, FastAPI, OpenTimelineIO (OTIO), CMX 3600 EDL, and Google Gemini API (gemini-3.1-flash-lite) with offline rule fallback.

Where AI Failed in Practice

  • Clock Math Hallucinations: LLMs failed at decimal-second durations (e.g. 01:12.40 to 01:45.10). Fix: Time arithmetic is 100% computed in integer milliseconds by deterministic code.
  • Confidence Inflation: Early prompts caused 90% of clips to score 88–95. Fix: Rubric-based scoring with explicit structural penalties.

6. Next Steps & Production Limitations

  1. Text-Only Screenplay Modality Limitation: PromoForge operates strictly on parsed timestamped screenplay text. Dialogue boundary safety is validated against script event start and end clocks, not direct audio waveform or acoustic phoneme analysis. Non-verbal visual acting beats and unscripted audio cues require human editor review.
  2. Audio-Visual Multimodal Grounding: Ingest proxy video alongside text screenplays to correlate dialogue with facial close-ups, vocal loudness spikes, and scene cuts via Whisper timestamps.
  3. Cross-Episode Multi-Segment Stitching: Expand the built-in createMultiSegmentPromo engine to automatically pair Episode 1 curiosity hooks with Episode 15 cliffhangers into seamless 45-second composite narrative teasers.
  4. Real-Time Retention Feedback Loop: Connect TikTok/Reels retention curve APIs via webhook to continuously tune hook scoring weights against real audience drop-off data.
